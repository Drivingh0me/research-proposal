\section{Overview}
Organic Solar Cell (OSC) research has experienced incredible advancements over the
previous 12 years \autocite{xuChallengingPM6likeDonor2023}
. Power Conversion Efficiency (PCE) of modern OSCs produced in
labs have exceeded 20\%, comparable to traditional silicon and perovskite cells2
. Much
of the advancement is credited to the development of PM6:Y6 donor:acceptor systems3
.
Current challenges in the development of commercially viable materials for OSCs are
stability4 and solubility in green solvents for deposition. This proposal is the
implementation of the Knoevenagel condensation5 in the preparation of PM6 and Y6
inspired green material libraries and the screening of these libraries for preferred
properties of irradiation stability and solubility in green solvents, followed by
implementation in bulk heterojunction OSCs to determine PCE.

The Knoevenagel condensation is an
aldol condensation that typically forms
conjugated systems. This reaction
often has the greatest yields in
aqueous sodium hydroxide solutions
with small quantities of organic solvent
present5. This condensation reaction
has a much lower environmental
impact than the Stille coupling
commonly used to synthesize conjugated materials for OSCs.
A combinatorial library of polymeric donor materials produced via the Knoevenagel
polymerization can be screened by irradiation with light, and extraction with green
solvents such as 2-methyl tetrahydrofuran. The surviving polymers can be examined for
PCE with multiple NFAs.
